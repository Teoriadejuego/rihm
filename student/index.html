Discuss a choice

Pick a question with a partner. Use one decision or one visible comparison as evidence, then listen for another explanation.

What counts as fair?

In D3 you receive 10 in either option. The other person could receive 10 or 18. What would someone who values equality choose? What would someone who values the total payoff choose?

How would you share a team bonus?

Imagine allocating a bonus after a group project. What would you need to know about effort, need and responsibility before deciding whether everyone should receive the same amount?

Who pays for a shared benefit?

Shading a school playground helps every family. How would you divide the cost between households with different incomes? Compare your rule with the one you used for the team bonus.

How far can a comparison take us?

Switch reference countries while keeping the same indicator. Which difference changes? Check who took part and how many responses were counted. What else would you need to know to explain the change?

How to read this experiment

Indicators overlap

Altruist, egalitarian, selfish and antisocial are overlapping indicators of decision patterns. A consistent response can match more than one, so their percentages can exceed 100% when added.

These labels describe choices in this task, not personality or moral worth.

Two denominators

The four social indicators and the compassion–envy matrix use consistent responses. Inconsistent uses complete binary responses. Incomplete and non-binary responses are excluded from public calculations.

Protected aggregates

Protected means a value was withheld before publication. Small counts, small denominators and small complements are protected; related cells may also be withheld. A protected value is never zero.

Filters select published aggregates. No individual answers are sent to this page.
