Work through the decisions

D1 · Would you give up two tickets?

Compare the two drawings. Which split gives the pair more tickets in total? Who pays for that gain?

Check the two totals

The equal split gives 20 tickets in total. The other split gives 24. You give up 2 tickets and the recipient gains 6.

D3 · What if you still receive ten?

Choose between 10 / 10 and 10 / 18, with your tickets listed first. Would you use the same rule as in D1?

See what changes

The recipient gains 8 tickets at no cost to you. The pair gains 8 too, but the gap between you grows. Which consideration matters most to you?

Which motives could fit the same decision?

In D2, choosing 10 / 10 over 12 / 4 preserves equality and gives the recipient more. Both reasons fit. The indicators below describe patterns across all six decisions.

Altruist

Favour gains for the recipient and the pair.

Egalitarian

Keep the two payoffs close.

Selfish

Favour your own payoff.

Antisocial

Reduce the other payoff or widen the gap.

How to read this experiment

Indicators overlap

Altruist, egalitarian, selfish and antisocial are overlapping indicators of decision patterns. A consistent response can match more than one, so their percentages can exceed 100% when added.

These labels describe choices in this task, not personality or moral worth.

Two denominators

The four social indicators and the compassion–envy matrix use consistent responses. Inconsistent uses complete binary responses. Incomplete and non-binary responses are excluded from public calculations.

Protected aggregates

Protected means a value was withheld before publication. Small counts, small denominators and small complements are protected; related cells may also be withheld. A protected value is never zero.

Filters select published aggregates. No individual answers are sent to this page.
